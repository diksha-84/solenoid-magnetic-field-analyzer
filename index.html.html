    Concept in short

    Biot–Savart law: every small piece of current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field. A solenoid is a stack of N circular loops, so the simulator adds up the field of every loop (exact elliptic-integral expression for a loop) at every point in space.

    Inside a long solenoid the field is almost uniform and equals μ₀nI (n = N/L). At the ends the field drops to about half of the centre value, because only half of the coil lies on one side of the end point. Outside, the field is weak and the lines close back from the N-pole to the S-pole.

    Right-hand rule: curl the fingers of your right hand along the current; the thumb points along B inside the coil (towards the N end). Increase I or N, or reduce L, to get a stronger field. A magnetic core multiplies the field by (about) μr.
